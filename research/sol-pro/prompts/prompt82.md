## Frozen modular definitions and strict boundary

Let `n : Nat`, `2<=n`, put `N=n+1`, and let

```text
a : Fin n -> Nat
```

be positive and injective. For every pivot `j : Fin n`, put

```text
p_j = a_j,
M_j = N*p_j,
rho_M(x) = min(x mod M, M-(x mod M)),
R_j = {r in {0,...,M_j-1} : N does not divide r},
B_i^j = {r in R_j : rho_(M_j)(r*a_i) < p_j}  for i != j,
mu_j(r) = #{i : Fin n | i != j and r in B_i^j}.
```

Every sum is owner-indexed. Coincident bad sets retain multiplicity. Original
speeds are positive and injective, but modular residues may repeat and need
not be units. Retain every gcd degeneration, reflection fixed point, antipode,
and excluded `N`-multiple.

Badness is strict `<p_j`; equality `rho=p_j` is safe. Never replace centered
cyclic distance by a one-sided interval. Every residue is its unique
representative in `0,...,M_j-1`.

The candidate-grid cardinality is

```text
|R_j| = n*p_j.
```

Prove it rather than treating it as a convention.

## Frozen saturated multiplicity transform

For a natural multiplicity `m`, define the rational number

```text
phi_8(m) = (1-m)/(8*m+1).
```

The numerator is interpreted in `Int` and the positive denominator in
`Nat`, then embedded in `Rat`. Thus

```text
phi_8(0)=1,
phi_8(1)=0,
phi_8(m)<0 for m>=2,
and -1/8 < phi_8(m) < 0 for m>=2.
```

Define the local and global scores

```text
S_j(a) = sum_(r in R_j) phi_8(mu_j(r)),
S_8(a) = sum_(j : Fin n) S_j(a).
```

The constant `8`, the denominator `8*m+1`, equal pivot weighting, and strict
target sign are frozen before the proof and search. Do not optimize the
constant after inspecting a tuple, add speed/gcd weights, take a positive
part, discard negative rows, or select a favorable subset of candidates.

