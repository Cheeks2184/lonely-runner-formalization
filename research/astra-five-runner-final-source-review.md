## Exact component hypotheses and derivation

one tag realizing all `u:Fin 3` quotient carries. Strict selector tests assign

between consecutive multiples of R; there is no denominator-coprimality
assumption or independent choice of the two nontrivial carries.

State top/residue/carry/digit theorems require only `m,x:Nat`, `x<5*5^m`,
`x%5=1 ∨ x%5=2` (plus the displayed finite u,k arguments). They allow m=0.
Euclidean division gives top `x/5^m`; the residue decoder gives `x%5`, and
the generic tag theorem supplies the coupled carries. Writing `R=5^m`,
`J=u.val+1`, `x=R*a+z`, the digit proof derives
`(J*x)/R=J*a+(J*z)/R`, then applies the selected-level correction at level
zero with current multiplier J. Its translation is exactly `J*k*(x%5)`.
The proof does not require the correction to be a unit at m=0.

the conditional hard theorem assumes exactly the universal finite carry
contract printed in the probe. It normalizes each d once and invokes that
contract once, yielding shared u,k and `A=J*(1+kR)`. The four old normalization
declarations retain their entire proof bytes and the crucial quantifier
order `∃ x, ... ∧ ∀ A, distance(A*x)=distance(A*d)`. Thus independent
coordinate complements preserve the same later A for all three speeds.

The selected digits give the three closed distances >=R. Because m>=1,

distance lemma handles all four nonzero digits, including digit 4 at equality.
The result uses `lambda=A%P`, `P=5R`; the unit representative theorem proves
