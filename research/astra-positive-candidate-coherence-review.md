## The hypothetical domain and exact endpoint windows

Let V be n>=3 positive distinct primitive integer speeds in a hypothetical
ordinary strict failure, minimal first by moving count and then primitive
sum. Set N=n+1, alpha=1/N, m=max V, S=V\{m}, Q=max S, and
`0<mu=max_t min_(v in V)||vt||<alpha`. Use the full closed deletion-good
set `W={t:||at||>=alpha for all a in S}`. Least count supplies an interval
in W. The accepted full-set contraction gives
`mt=k+e`, `|e|<=mu_m=mu-m(alpha-mu)/Q`, with 0<mu_m<mu<alpha.
Thus every point of W belongs to a unique nearest-index portion W_k inside
an original open m-alpha arc. K contains all these attained indices, with
no component or boundary omitted.

For a retained a, let R_a(k) be the residue of ak in [0,m). It is nonzero
at every k in K. Otherwise at an actual t in W_k,
`||at||<=a|e|/m<alpha`, contradicting a-goodness. Equivalently, if k/m
reduces to denominator q, no retained speed is divisible by q. This does
not assume the collision center itself is in W.

For the left endpoint L=k/m-alpha/m, every original covering arc has label
a<m and center l/a strictly left of k/m, by the accepted maximum-owner
privacy argument. Put D=ak-ml>0. Its signed residual at L is
`(D-a alpha)/m`, so open coverage is exactly `|ND-a|<m`. Since a<m and
D>0, the lower inequality is automatic. The upper inequality gives
`D<(m+a)/N<m`. Because D is congruent to ak modulo m, it is uniquely
R_a(k). Conversely each residue in that window defines the integral lift
l=(ak-D)/m and satisfies the original open endpoint coverage condition.

At the right endpoint the positive determinant mr-ak is congruent to
-ak modulo m and, in the same range, equals m-R_a(k). Thus the complete
sets of endpoint labels are exactly

`A_k^-={a in S:0<R_a(k)<(m+a)/N}`,
`A_k^+={a in S:0<m-R_a(k)<(m+a)/N}`.

There are no other numerator lifts in these windows. Same-speed arcs
are disjoint, so one label cannot hide multiple arcs at an endpoint.
All inequalities are strict because these are open covers. The two label
sets are disjoint: membership in both would imply
`m<2(m+a)/N<4m/N<=m`. They are nonempty because the full open alpha-family
covers both endpoints and no m-arc covers its own endpoints.

The candidate set is the full positive image
`C_k={|a+b-m|:a in A_k^-, b in A_k^+, a+b!=m}`. The accepted cancellation
holds on every point of W_k, with bound
`||ut||<alpha-[(m-max(a,b))/m](alpha-mu_m)<alpha`.
Hence every positive candidate is fresh: a retained u would be alpha-good
on the nonempty W_k. The raw sum formula alone does not prove this fact.
Consequently all C_k lie in the common actual hole set
`H={1,...,m-1}\S`, whose size is (m-1)-(n-1)=m-n.

In the branch where every C_k is nonempty but their intersection is empty,
H cannot be empty or a singleton. Thus |H|>=2 and m>=n+2. If a common
positive candidate existed, it would be bad on all W; outside W an old
retained label is bad. Continuity and compactness would give a strict
replacement failure, and primitive normalization would preserve count and
lower sum. This is the accepted conditional descent, not a selection of
such a candidate.

## One rational witness and the exact reflection restriction

The reduced denominator q cannot be 1, since every retained speed would
then be divisible by q. For q>=2, absence of retained q-multiples implies
`||ac||>=1/q` for all a in S, at c=k/m. At the single common time c+s,
where `s=1/[q(m+Q)]`, the m-phase is an integer plus
`ms=m/[q(m+Q)]<1/q<=1/2`; its norm is exactly ms. For every retained a,

`||a(c+s)||>=1/q-a s>=1/q-Qs=m/[q(m+Q)]`.

Thus the actual maximum satisfies

`mu>=m/[q(m+Q)]`,
`q>=m/[mu(m+Q)]>Nm/(m+Q)>N/2`.

The first inequality is weak and the last two are strict, using mu<1/N
and Q<m. No favorable private-center phase or unavailable denominator
speed was supplied. This is a necessary restriction on every attained
maximum collision.

Reflection t->-t preserves all retained norms and the whole W. Uniqueness
of the nearest index under the contracted bound maps all of W_k onto
W_(m-k). The original endpoint arcs reflect to original arcs with the same
labels and exchange left with right. Equivalently,
`R_a(m-k)=m-R_a(k)` for the nonzero residues above. Therefore

`K=-K modulo m`, `C_(m-k)=C_k`.

A fixed index solves 2k=0 modulo m and has reduced denominator 1 or 2.
The strict q>N/2 bound excludes both, including N=4 where q>2. Thus K is
a union of distinct two-element reflection orbits. With only one orbit,
the two nonempty candidate sets would be identical and their intersection
nonempty. Positive incoherence consequently requires at least two orbits
and |K|>=4, as well as the two-hole condition above.

These conclusions do not give intersections between different orbits,
interval structure of the candidate sets, or a Helly-type rule. The
minimum-sum replacement witness for a local candidate u lies in some
portion where u is not a candidate, but supplies no decreasing sequence
of candidates or common choice. Reflection only identifies the two sets
within each orbit.
