# Pass 52: the complete two-divisible boundary implication

The boundary-minimum-zero theorem is now a Lean proof with the exact hypotheses
of the reviewed manuscript. For positive natural speeds on five labels, it
assumes exactly two speeds divisible by three, one of those divisible by six,
at most three even labels, and absence of a closed sixth-margin witness at
any real time. At every real time where a specified exceptional label has
fractional phase 5/6, the two divisible labels have minimum norm zero.

## Proof structure

A finite anchored fiber has an attained pair maximum, which may initially be
zero. A maximum at least 1/6 gives a safe divisible seed and a special
exceptional phase; a common third shift contradicts the nonexistence premise.
If the maximum is positive but below 1/6, order the two norms and obtain the
checked folded-band packet. The swapped ordering carries the complete fiber
bound and divisibility data with it.

For the regular residue cases, five explicit rows choose one affine time
nτ+σ/6 with n in {1,2,3} and σ in {1,-1}. Both divisible labels are safe at
that same time, while the anchor has phase 1/6 or 5/6. Both signs are included.
This supplies the same common-third-shift contradiction.

If both pair speeds are divisible by six and the anchor is even, the three
specified labels exhaust the even labels. The two remaining labels are odd.
The folded bands and anchor phase make the three even labels safe at each of
2τ, 2τ+1/2 and 4τ+1/2. The unconditional two-phase choice selects one of
those times for both odd labels. Thus all five labels share a witness.

The maximum is consequently zero. The original all-real fiber bound then
proves zero minimum at the arbitrary queried time, rather than only at the
chosen maximizer. Five public declarations cover the odd half-time identity,
affine selector, two-odd-label complement, three-time even-anchor witness and
final boundary theorem. The last theorem does not assume primitivity or a
supplied cover, and does not claim a positive time in an intermediate rescue.
