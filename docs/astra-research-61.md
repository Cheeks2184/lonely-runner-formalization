This pass records structural progress and precise failures for the arbitrary-N
deletion-seed mechanism, alongside the scheduled Terra High delivery review.
No new Lean source or successor-runner theorem is introduced.

Within a fixed deletion phase cell, lowering the margin can only transfer a
left or right endpoint to a faster speed. Its active-owner inequalities give
an acyclic relation on one side with the deletion and cell held fixed. This
independently reviewed lemma does not compare the endpoint owner with the
deleted runner or supply a new strong seed after changing that deletion.

Two tempting local transitions fail. An unbounded integer-speed family has a
trapped seed whose two first pivot-entry times each leave two old runners bad.
A separate exact example has adjacent trapped seeds for different deletions,
but their facing endpoint owner is neither next deleted runner; permitting
both improving directions gives a velocity cycle. Both examples have good
seeds elsewhere. They refute the stated local rules while leaving a proof
using simultaneous global trapping open.

A valid contact lemma gives another possible way to refute the auxiliary
seed criterion: a full witness with at least two boundary contacts from each
direction is isolated in every deletion's weak-good set and cannot belong to
a protected seed interval. To make an auxiliary counterexample with known
full witnesses, this pattern would have to hold for every full witness, and
an actual full witness would need to be supplied separately. The one tuple
constructed to test that pattern fails: it has four isolated grid witnesses,
six positive-length full-good components and a full stronger-margin witness
at 45/88. Its complete exact output is retained, with no expanded search.

The stronger published MSS subset-gcd condition also received a scope screen.
The project has formalized its maximum-height consequence, not that full
geometric refinement. The existing consecutive-block arithmetic example
satisfies even the subset-gcd bound for every N>=5. Thus adopting that bound
alone would not fix the arithmetic-only route. This comparison is manuscript
work; neither a new kernel theorem nor a full formalization of the published
geometric argument is claimed.
